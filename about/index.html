Extracting Historical Semantic Knowledge & Repurposing It for Exact Symbolic Reasoning

About This Edition¶

geometor • proclus reconstructs the monumental philosophical, axiomatic, and historical commentary of Proclus Lycius Diadochus (412–485 AD), head (Diadochus) of the Neoplatonic Academy of Athens: In Primum Euclidis Elementorum Librum Commentarii (Commentary on the First Book of Euclid’s Elements, c. 470 AD). Proclus’s treatise stands at the crossroads of the ancient exact sciences, preserving the fountainhead of early Greek geometry—including the celebrated Eudemian Summary (Friedlein 64–70, the sole surviving fragment of Eudemus of Rhodes’s fourth-century BC History of Geometry), Pappus’s zero-construction proof of Proposition I.5, Oenopides’s astronomical gnomon, Thales’s theorems, Claudius Ptolemy’s monograph on parallel lines (Friedlein 365–368), the Pythagorean angle-sum proof, and the invention of the geometric application of areas (parabole, hyperbole, elleipsis).

The codex extracts the implicit structural architecture of Proclus’s work across seven comprehensive units: Prologue Part I (mathematical ontology and noetics), Prologue Part II (the nature of geometry and the Eudemian Summary), Definitions, Postulates and Axioms, Propositions I.1–15, Propositions I.16–32, and Propositions I.33–48. Powered by photon-platform/publish, each section is synchronized across four tracks: Thomas Taylor’s pioneering English translation (1788–1792), Gottfried Friedlein’s 1873 critical Teubner Greek text, argumentative summaries, and philological notes keyed directly to Friedlein page-and-line coordinates and calibrated diagrams.

Within the GEOMETOR ecosystem, Proclus serves as the indispensable epistemological bridge between synthetic antiquity and modern exact computation. His commentary furnishes over 150 foundational citations across Sir Thomas Heath’s edition of Euclid's Elements (geometor/euclid & geometor/elements); provides the philosophical foundation for Fr. Peter Hoenen S.J.'s inquiry into mathematical imagination (phantasia), intelligible matter (hyle noete), and projective intuition in De Noetica Geometriae; and articulates the Six Canonical Parts of a Geometric Proposition (Protasis, Ekthesis, Diorismos, Kataskeue, Apodeixis, Symperasma, Friedlein 203–207), providing the classical deductive grammar for modern constructive verification.

The GEOMETOR Mission¶

The GEOMETOR project, initiated and directed by phi ARCHITECT, is dedicated to the complete digital renaissance and computational re-grounding of classical geometry and axiomatic thought. The mission advances across three foundational pillars:

  1. Extracting Historical Semantic Knowledge: Moving beyond passive page scans, static PDFs, and raw OCR to recover the living deductive structure of 2,300+ years of geometric, axiomatic, and philosophical treatises. GEOMETOR elevates historical prose, marginal scholia, primary-source folios (via publish folio directives), and calibrated Ink-to-Vector .euclid-graphic diagrams into structured, machine-addressable semantic graphs. Each proposition, enunciation, step, hypothesis, and proof is rendered uniquely identifiable, computationally navigable, and interoperable across the entire historical canon.

  2. Exact Symbolic Grounding (``geometor.model``): Anchoring geometric constructions and spatial relations in 100% exact symbolic algebra via SymPy and Norman J. Wildberger’s Rational Trigonometry invariants (quadrance, spread, cross-ratio). By operating strictly over exact algebraic number fields rather than floating-point approximations, GEOMETOR eliminates rounding degradation, preserves absolute mathematical truth, and guarantees that every geometric demonstration is provably exact.

  3. Repurposing Knowledge Across Mediums: Re-deploying extracted semantic and algebraic structures across synchronized multi-track Book Codices and accessible EPUB 3 editions (powered by photon-platform/publish), computable deductive canons (geometor.elements, geometor.etc, geometor.pappus, geometor.divine), interactive SVG exploratory laboratories (geometor.explorer), ultra-high-definition visual broadcasts, and neuro-symbolic program synthesis (geometor.seer) across geometor.com.

Sources, Provenance & Methodology¶

This edition adheres to rigorous Digital Scriptorium standards and authentic public-domain witnesses:

  • Critical Greek Text: Gottfried Friedlein’s landmark critical edition, Procli Diadochi in primum Euclidis Elementorum librum commentarii (Leipzig: B. G. Teubner, 1873, pp. 1–436; CTS urn:cts:greekLit:tlg4036.tlg011.opp-grc1), collated from Codex Monacensis Graecus 427 (M) against the 1533 Basel editio princeps of Simon Grynaeus and the 1560 Latin translation of Francesco Barozzi (Barocius).

  • English Textual Witness: Thomas Taylor’s unabridged English translation, The Philosophical and Mathematical Commentaries of Proclus on the First Book of Euclid’s Elements (two volumes, London, 1788–1789, reissued 1792; Project Gutenberg #74253 and #79455), corrected for typographical errata and modernized in technical vocabulary.

  • Strict Philological Integrity: The codex preserves the ancient text without embedding modern executable code, linking outward via canonical Friedlein anchors and GEOMETOR concordance entries to sister repositories.
