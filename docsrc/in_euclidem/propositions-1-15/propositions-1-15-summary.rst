Commentary on Propositions I.1–I.15: Triangles, Congruence & Perpendiculars — Analytical Summary
================================================================================================

*Scope*: **Friedlein pp. 208–305** · *Book III (Propositions I–XV & Porisms)*

Architectural Argument of Propositions I.1–I.15
-----------------------------------------------

In the first fifteen propositions of Book I (*Friedlein* pp. 208–305), :person:`Euclid <euclid>` establishes the elementary constructive toolkit, fundamental triangle congruence laws, bisections, perpendiculars, and linear angle relations—all without ever invoking parallel lines:

1. **Constructive Foundations & Zeno's Critique (:just:`I.1`–:just:`I.3`, *Friedlein* 208–232)**:
   - :just:`Prop. I.1 <I.1>` opens the *Elements* by constructing the equilateral triangle, the most symmetric rectilinear archetype, and serves as Proclus's live laboratory for the Six Canonical Parts, :term:`lemma <lemma>`, :term:`case <ptosis>`, :term:`porism <porisma>`, :term:`objection <enstasis>`, and :term:`reduction <apagoge>` (*Friedlein* 208–213). Proclus also preserves :person:`Zeno of Sidon's <zeno-of-sidon>` incisive Epicurean objection—that two straight lines might share a common segment—together with :person:`Posidonius's <posidonius>` reply (*Friedlein* 214–218).
   - :just:`Props. I.2–I.3 <I.2>` emancipate the compass from collapsing by transferring and subtracting segments across arbitrary positions; Proclus enumerates the positional cases omitted by Euclid.
2. **First Congruence, Isosceles Symmetry & Conversion Theory (:just:`I.4`–:just:`I.8`, *Friedlein* 233–269)**:
   - :just:`Prop. I.4 <I.4>` (SAS) is the first *theorem* in Euclid, proved via :term:`superposition <epharmoge>`; Proclus appends the debate between :person:`Carpus of Antioch <carpus>` and :person:`Geminus <geminus>` on whether problems precede theorems (*Friedlein* 241–243).
   - :just:`Prop. I.5 <I.5>` (*pons asinorum*) records :person:`Thales's <thales>` discovery alongside :person:`Pappus's <pappus>` astonishing zero-construction proof (*Friedlein* 249–250), comparing $\triangle ABC$ directly with its mirror orientation $\triangle ACB$!
   - :just:`Prop. I.6 <I.6>` occasions Proclus's master-analysis of :term:`geometric conversion <antistrophe>` (Menaechmus and Amphinomus) and :term:`reductio ad absurdum <adynaton>` (*Friedlein* 252–256).
   - :just:`Props. I.7–I.8 <I.8>` establish SSS congruence, enriched with :person:`Philo of Byzantium's <philo>` elegant three-case proof bypassing Lemma I.7 (*Friedlein* 266–268).
3. **Bisections, Perpendiculars, Vertical Angles & Porisms (:just:`I.9`–:just:`I.15`, *Friedlein* 270–305)**:
   - :just:`Props. I.9–I.10 <I.9>` bisect any rectilinear angle and finite segment—explaining why **angle trisection** requires higher curves (conchoids of :person:`Nicomedes <nicomedes>`, quadratrices of :person:`Hippias <hippias>`, spirals of :person:`Archimedes <archimedes>`, *Friedlein* 272) and why universal bisectability refutes :person:`Xenocrates' <xenocrates>` *indivisible lines* (*Friedlein* 277–279).
   - :just:`Props. I.11–I.12 <I.12>` erect and drop perpendiculars, recording :person:`Apollonius's <apollonius>` constructions and :person:`Oenopides of Chios's <oenopides>` astronomical term :term:`kata gnomona <gnomon>` (*Friedlein* 283).
   - :just:`Props. I.13–I.15 <I.15>` establish supplementary and vertical angles (discovered by :person:`Thales <thales>`, proved scientifically by Euclid, *Friedlein* 299) and conclude with Proclus's famous **Excursus on Porisms** (*Friedlein* 301–305).
