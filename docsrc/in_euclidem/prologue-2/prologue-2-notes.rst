Prologue Part II: Philosophy & History of Geometry — GEOMETOR Commentary & Concordance Notes
============================================================================================

GEOMETOR Philosophical & Mathematical Commentary
------------------------------------------------

1. ***Phantasia*, *Probole*, and *Materia Intelligibilis* (`Friedlein 48–57, 78–79`)**:
   Proclus's identification of :term:`mathematical imagination <phantasia>` with :person:`Aristotle's <aristotle>` :term:`intelligible matter <hyle-noete>` (*ὕλη νοητή*, `Friedlein 49–53`) is one of the central pillars of :person:`Hoenen's <hoenen>` :cite:`De Noetica Geometriae <hoenen:1954>` (Caput I §1; Caput III, p. 67, citing `Friedlein 49, 12–17` and `49, 17 sqq.`; Caput IV *passim*). Where pure :term:`dianoia <dianoia>` holds the unextended universal *logos* of the circle, :term:`probole <probole>` projects that *logos* onto the spatial continuum of *phantasia*, generating plural, intersecting circles and segments—the exact cognitive counterpart of a constructive geometric graph!
2. **The Eudemian Summary (`Friedlein 64–70`) & The Divine Proportion**:
   Notice at `Friedlein 67, 6` (cited twice in `geometor/euclid`, Vol. I p. 137 & Vol. II p. 99 on :just:`IV.10`) Proclus's report from :person:`Eudemus <eudemus>` that :person:`Eudoxus of Cnidus <eudoxus>` *"multiplied the number of theorems concerning **the section** (περὶ τὴν τομήν) which had their origin in :person:`Plato <plato>`, applying the analytical method to them."* Following Bretschneider, Tannery, and :person:`Heath <heath>`, *"the section"* par excellence is the **golden section** (extreme and mean ratio, $\phi$), linking the Academy's analytical inquiries directly to Books II (:just:`II.11`), IV (:just:`IV.10`–:just:`IV.11`), VI (:just:`VI.30`), and XIII (icosahedron & dodecahedron)—the guiding thread of **GEOMETOR** (`geometor/divine`).
3. **Cross-Reference to the Six Parts of a Proposition**:
   While Chaps. V–IX of Prologue II (*Friedlein* 70–84) establish the architecture of :term:`stoicheiosis <stoicheiosis>`, :term:`problems <problema>`, and :term:`theorems <theorema>`, Proclus places his detailed analysis of the **Six Canonical Parts of a Geometric Proposition** (*Protasis, Ekthesis, Diorismos, Kataskeue, Apodeixis, Symperasma*) immediately after the Common Notions at `Friedlein 203–207` (see :ref:`sec-six-parts` in :doc:`../postulates-axioms/postulates-axioms-en`).
