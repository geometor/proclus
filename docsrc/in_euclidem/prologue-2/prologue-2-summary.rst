Prologue Part II: Philosophy & History of Geometry — Analytical Summary
=======================================================================

*Scope*: **Friedlein pp. 48–84** · *Book II (Chaps. I–IX)*

Architectural Argument of Prologue Part II
------------------------------------------

Turning from universal mathematics to **geometry proper**, **Prologue Part II** (*Friedlein* pp. 48–84; Taylor Book II) resolves the central epistemological paradox of geometry, preserves the foundational history of Greek mathematics, and defines the deductive purpose of :person:`Euclid's <euclid>` *Elements*:

1. **Mathematical Imagination (*Phantasia*) & *Probole* (*Friedlein* 48–63)**: How can geometry speak of *two* equal circles, bisect a line, or superpose one triangle upon another if the pure concept (*logos*) of the circle or triangle in :term:`dianoia <dianoia>` is unique, indivisible, and unextended? Proclus answers with his celebrated doctrine of :term:`projection <probole>`: *dianoia* projects its partless internal *logoi* outward onto the luminous screen of :term:`mathematical imagination <phantasia>`, which serves as :term:`intelligible matter <hyle-noete>` (*ὕλη νοητή*). Like a thinker beholding his own reflection in a clear mirror, the geometer constructs, divides, and compares extended yet sensible-free figures in *phantasia* while reasoning universally from the *logos* within *dianoia*.
2. **The Eudemian Summary of Greek Geometry (*Friedlein* 64–70)**: Excerpting :person:`Eudemus of Rhodes's <eudemus>` lost :cite:`History of Geometry <eudemus:history-of-geometry>`, Proclus traces the ascent of geometry from sensible measurement along the Nile to pure science:
   - :person:`Thales of Miletus <thales>` brings geometry from Egypt to Greece; :person:`Mamercus <mamercus>` wins renown; :person:`Pythagoras <pythagoras>` elevates geometry into liberal philosophy, discovering proportionals/irrationals and the cosmic figures.
   - :person:`Anaxagoras <anaxagoras>` and :person:`Oenopides of Chios <oenopides>` advance astronomical geometry; :person:`Hippocrates of Chios <hippocrates-of-chios>` squares lunes and composes the first *Elements*, alongside :person:`Theodorus of Cyrene <theodorus>`.
   - :person:`Plato <plato>` inspires an explosion of discovery in the Academy: :person:`Leodamas of Thasos <leodamas>`, :person:`Archytas of Tarentum <archytas>`, and :person:`Theaetetus <theaetetus>`; :person:`Neoclides <neoclides>` and :person:`Leon <leon>` (inventor of :term:`diorismoi <diorismos>`); :person:`Eudoxus of Cnidus <eudoxus>` (general theorems, proportions, and *the section* via :term:`analysis <analysis>`); :person:`Amyclas <amyclas>`, :person:`Menaechmus <menaechmus>`, :person:`Dinostratus <dinostratus>`, :person:`Theudius of Magnesia <theudius>`, :person:`Athenaeus <athenaeus>`, :person:`Hermotimus <hermotimus>`, and :person:`Philippus of Medma <philippus>`.
   - Finally, :person:`Euclid <euclid>`—living under Ptolemy I ("*there is no royal road to geometry*")—systematizes the *Elements*, perfecting Eudoxus's theorems, Theaetetus's irrationals, and bringing earlier loose proofs to irrefragable demonstration.
3. **Purpose (*Skopos*) & *Stoicheiosis* (*Friedlein* 70–84)**: Proclus distinguishes the cosmic *skopos* of the whole thirteen-book *Elements* (constructing the five Platonic solids of the :cite:`Timaeus <plato:timaeus:31c-36d>`) from its pedagogical *skopos* as an :term:`elementary system <stoicheiosis>` training the intellect in rigorous :term:`theorems <theorema>` and :term:`problems <problema>`. Note also how Prologue II's discussion of first principles and propositions (*Friedlein* 75–81) prepares directly for Proclus's formal analysis of the **Six Canonical Parts of a Geometric Proposition** at *Friedlein* 203–207 (:ref:`sec-six-parts`).
