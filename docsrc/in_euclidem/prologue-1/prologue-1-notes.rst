Prologue Part I: Mathematical Being & Common Science — GEOMETOR Commentary & Concordance Notes
==============================================================================================

GEOMETOR Philosophical & Mathematical Commentary
------------------------------------------------

1. **Proclus vs. Aristotle on Abstraction (`Friedlein 12–18`) and Hoenen's Synthesis**:
   In `geometor/hoenen` (:cite:`De Noetica Geometriae <hoenen:1954>`, Caput III, p. 67), :person:`Petrus Hoenen <hoenen>` directly cites `Friedlein 12, 14–22` as the locus classicus of antiquity's critique of naive empiricism in geometry. Proclus asks how exactitude (*ἀκρίβεια*) and necessary demonstration could ever be extracted from sensible bodies that are continually shifting and never realize an indivisible point or breadthless line. Hoenen uses Proclus's objection to distinguish *empirical induction* from genuine Scholastic-noetic *insight into intelligible extension* in the phantasm—showing a deep structural convergence between Proclus's critique of :term:`hysterogenes <hysterogenes>` empiricism and Hoenen's critique of positivist-empiricist theories of geometry.
2. **Exact Symbolic Algebra in GEOMETOR (`geometor/model`)**:
   Proclus's insistence that mathematical entities preserve exact ratios governed by :term:`Limit <peras>` and :term:`the Unlimited <apeiron>` (*Friedlein* 5–7) rather than approximate sensory measurements anticipates **GEOMETOR's symbolic foundation**: in `geometor/model`, geometric constructions preserve exact algebraic identities ($\sqrt{2}, \sqrt{3}, \phi = \frac{1+\sqrt{5}}{2}$) rather than floating-point approximations.
3. **Textual Transmission Note**:
   Thomas Taylor's 1788 translation of Book I followed Simon Grynaeus's :cite:`1533 Basel Greek text <grynaeus:1533>` alongside :person:`Franciscus Barocius's <barocius>` :cite:`1560 Latin edition <barocius:1560>`, while our synchronized Greek track presents :person:`Gottfried Friedlein's <friedlein>` :cite:`1873 Teubner critical text <friedlein:1873>` (*pp. 3–47*).
