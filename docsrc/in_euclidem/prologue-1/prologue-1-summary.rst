Prologue Part I: Mathematical Being & Common Science — Analytical Summary
=========================================================================

*Scope*: **Friedlein pp. 3–47** · *Book I (Chaps. I–XV)*

Architectural Argument of Prologue Part I
-----------------------------------------

In the fifteen chapters of **Prologue Part I** (*Friedlein* pp. 3–47; Taylor Book I), :person:`Proclus <proclus>` constructs the most complete surviving treatise of late-antique **philosophy of mathematics**. Before examining geometry in its specific spatial character, he establishes the *common science of mathematics* (*κοινὴ μαθηματικὴ ἐπιστήμη*) across five interlocking movements:

1. **Intermediate Ontology (*Friedlein* 3–5)**: Drawing upon :person:`Plato's <plato>` Divided Line (:cite:`Republic VI, 509d–511e <plato:rep:VI.510b-511d>`), Proclus situates :term:`mathematical essence <ousia-mathematike>` in the exact :term:`middle rank <metaxy>` of reality. Above it stand the simple, unextended, self-identical intelligibles grasped by intuitive :term:`Intellect <nous>`; below it lie shifting, material, inexact sensible bodies grasped by sense and :term:`opinion <doxa>`. Mathematical objects surpass sensibles in eternal exactitude and necessity, yet unfold into plurality, division, and relation unlike pure Forms.
2. **Limit and the Unlimited (*Friedlein* 5–7)**: Following :person:`Plato's <plato>` :cite:`Philebus <plato:philebus:16c-26d>`, Proclus derives all mathematical existence from the cosmic dyad of :term:`Limit <peras>` and :term:`the Unlimited <apeiron>`. Limit bestows definite ratio, boundary, commensurability, and form; the Unlimited supplies inexhaustible progression in number and continuous divisibility in magnitude.
3. **Common Axioms and Critique of Abstraction (*Friedlein* 7–19)**: Theorems of proportion, equality, whole-and-part, and sameness-and-difference pervade arithmetic, geometry, music, and astronomy alike because they descend from a single prior mathematical science. Against Peripatetic empiricism, Proclus argues (*Friedlein* 12–18) that mathematical concepts cannot be :term:`later-born abstractions <hysterogenes>` gleaned from sensible bodies: sense-objects never exhibit an indivisible point, a breadthless line, or exact equality, nor could mutable matter ever ground eternal apodictic demonstration. Instead, :term:`discursive understanding <dianoia>` awakens substantial *logoi* innate within the soul.
4. **The Ascent of the Soul & Division of the Sciences (*Friedlein* 19–42)**: Defending theoretical mathematics against utilitarian and Epicurean detractors, Proclus synthesizes two great ancient classifications of the mathematical sciences:
   - The **Pythagorean Quadrivium** (*Friedlein* 35–37), dividing Quantity (*ποσόν*) into discrete (*ἀριθμητική* per se, *μουσική* in relation) and Magnitude (*πηλίκον*) into stationary (*γεωμετρία*) and moving (*σφαιρική / ἀστρονομία*).
   - **Geminus's Encyclopedic Classification** (*Friedlein* 38–42), contrasting pure sciences of intelligibles (arithmetic and geometry) with six sciences operating upon sensibles: mechanics, astronomy, optics, geodesy, canonics, and logistic.
5. **Dialectic and *Mathesis* as Recollection (*Friedlein* 42–47)**: In strict fidelity to :cite:`Republic VII <plato:rep:VII.525a-534e>` and the :cite:`Meno <plato:meno:81e-85d>`, Platonic Dialectic crowns all mathematics as its unifying *thrigkos* (coping-stone), furnishing the unhypothetical light by which *mathesis* becomes true :term:`recollection <anamnesis>`.
