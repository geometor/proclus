Commentary on Propositions I.33–I.48: Areas, Application & Pythagoras — Analytical Summary
==========================================================================================

*Scope*: **Friedlein pp. 384–436** · *Book IV (Propositions XXXIII–XLVIII)*

Architectural Argument of Propositions I.33–I.48
------------------------------------------------

In the grand finale of Book I (*Friedlein* pp. 384–432), :person:`Euclid <euclid>` ascends from congruence to **equivalence of area**, culminating in the **Pythagorean Application of Areas** and **the Theorem of Pythagoras**:

1. **Parallelograms & The Birth of Locus Theorems (:just:`I.33`–:just:`I.41`, *Friedlein* 384–415)**:
   - :just:`Props. I.33–I.34 <I.34>` bridge parallel lines and quadrilateral figures, generating the *parallelogrammic area* (*παραλληλόγραμμον χωρίον*) and proving its bisection by the diameter.
   - :just:`Prop. I.35 <I.35>` (*parallelograms on the same base and in the same parallels are equal*) inaugurates a new level of geometric equality—*equality of area without congruence of form*—and is hailed by Proclus as the first **locus theorem** (:term:`τοπικὸν θεώρημα <topikon-theorema>`, *Friedlein* 394–396), since the upper side may slide infinitely along the parallel locus while preserving constant area even as perimeter grows without bound!
   - :just:`Props. I.36–I.41 <I.41>` extend area invariance to parallelograms and triangles on equal bases, examine partial converses (:just:`I.39`–:just:`I.40`), and establish that a parallelogram is double a triangle sharing its base and parallels (:just:`I.41`), to which Proclus adds an original exercise on the area of trapezia (*Friedlein* 414–415).
2. **Complements & The Pythagorean Application of Areas (:just:`I.42`–:just:`I.45`, *Friedlein* 415–423)**:
   - :just:`Prop. I.43 <I.43>` proves the equality of the **complements** (*παραπληρώματα*) about the diameter of any parallelogram—the algebraic engine of Greek geometric transformation.
   - On :just:`Prop. I.44 <I.44>`, Proclus preserves one of the jewels of the history of mathematics (*Friedlein* 419–420): :person:`Eudemus's <eudemus>` testimony that **application of areas** (:term:`παραβολή <parabole>`), **exceeding** (:term:`ὑπερβολή <hyperbole>`), and **falling-short** (:term:`ἔλλειψις <elleipsis>`) are *"ancient discoveries of the Muse of the Pythagoreans"*, providing the foundation for Book II geometric algebra, Book VI quadratic equations, and :person:`Apollonius's <apollonius>` naming of the three conic sections!
3. **The Square & The Theorem of Pythagoras (:just:`I.46`–:just:`I.48`, *Friedlein* 423–432)**:
   - :just:`Prop. I.46 <I.46>` constructs the square on any segment.
   - On :just:`Prop. I.47 <I.47>`, Proclus treats the anecdote of :person:`Pythagoras's <pythagoras>` ox-sacrifice with scholarly circumspection (*Friedlein* 426, 6–9), expressing supreme admiration for **Euclid himself** both for the luminous Book I area-shearing demonstration (via the famous "windmill" configuration of :just:`I.4`, :just:`I.41`, and :just:`I.46`) and for generalizing the theorem in Book VI (:just:`VI.31`) to arbitrary similar figures! He also records the two classical integer right-triangle generators (*Friedlein* 428–429): the **Pythagorean odd-number rule** and the **Platonic even-number rule**.
   - :just:`Prop. I.48 <I.48>` proves the converse of I.47 and closes the *Commentary* (*Friedlein* 432) with a tribute to :person:`Syrianus <syrianus>` and an invitation to future philosophical commentators on the remaining books of Euclid.
