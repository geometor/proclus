Commentary on Propositions I.16–I.32: Inequalities, Parallels & Angle-Sum — Analytical Summary
==============================================================================================

*Scope*: **Friedlein pp. 305–384** · *Book III (Props. XVI–XXVI) & Book IV (Props. XXVII–XXXII)*

Architectural Argument of Propositions I.16–I.32
------------------------------------------------

Spanning the second half of Proclus's Book III and the dramatic opening of Book IV (*Friedlein* pp. 305–384), **Propositions I.16–I.32** carry Euclidean geometry from **absolute (neutral) triangle inequalities** across the **watershed of the Parallel Postulate** to the **universal interior angle-sum of the triangle**:

1. **Neutral Geometry of Inequalities & Triangle Determination (:just:`I.16`–:just:`I.26`, *Friedlein* 305–353)**:
   - :just:`Props. I.16–I.17 <I.16>` prove without Postulate 5 that an exterior angle exceeds either remote interior angle and that any two angles of a triangle are less than two right angles.
   - :just:`Props. I.18–I.21 <I.20>` pair greater sides with greater angles and prove the **Triangle Inequality** (:just:`I.20 <I.20>`). When Epicureans mock I.20 as *"evident even to an ass walking straight to its hay"* (*Friedlein* 322), Proclus replies that sense perceives only *that* (*τὸ ὅτι*), whereas science demands *why* (*τὸ διότι*), and preserves :person:`Heron's <heron>` and :person:`Porphyry's <porphyry>` alternative proofs (*Friedlein* 323–326).
   - :just:`Props. I.22–I.23 <I.22>` construct triangles from three given segments—vindicating the necessity of the :term:`diorismos <diorismos>`—and copy any rectilinear angle (attributed by :person:`Eudemus <eudemus>` to :person:`Oenopides <oenopides>`, *Friedlein* 333).
   - :just:`Props. I.24–I.25 <I.24>` establish the Hinge Theorems (enriched with :person:`Menelaus's <menelaus>` direct proof, *Friedlein* 345–347).
   - :just:`Prop. I.26 <I.26>` completes triangle congruence (ASA and AAS), featuring Porphyry's exhaustive combinatorial proof of why SSA and AAA fail (*Friedlein* 351) and Eudemus's report that :person:`Thales <thales>` used I.26 to determine **the distance of ships at sea** (*Friedlein* 352, 14–18).
2. **Theory of Parallels & The Attempted Proofs of Postulate 5 (:just:`I.27`–:just:`I.31`, *Friedlein* 354–376)**:
   - :just:`Props. I.27–I.28 <I.27>` establish sufficient conditions for parallel lines without Postulate 5.
   - At :just:`Prop. I.29 <I.29>`—the exact moment Euclid first invokes :just:`Postulate 5 <I.post.5>`—Proclus inserts his monumental nine-page critical excursion (*Friedlein* 365–373): (a) summarizing :person:`Claudius Ptolemy's <ptolemy>` lost treatise attempting to prove I.29 and Postulate 5; (b) exposing the hidden symmetry fallacy in Ptolemy's argument; (c) refuting a Zeno-like infinite-bisection sophism; and (d) presenting **Proclus's own attempted proof**, explicitly assuming :person:`Aristotle's <aristotle>` lemma that diverging rays separate beyond any finite bound—thereby isolating bounded equidistance as an exact logical equivalent of the Parallel Postulate!
3. **The Triangle Angle-Sum & Pythagorean Demonstration (:just:`I.32`, *Friedlein* 376–384)**:
   - :just:`Prop. I.32 <I.32>` proves that the exterior angle equals the two remote interiors and the three interior angles equal two right angles. Proclus preserves **the ancient Pythagorean proof** via :person:`Eudemus <eudemus>` (*Friedlein* 379, 2–15), analyzes :person:`Aristotle's <aristotle>` doctrine of commensurately universal (*καθόλου πρῶτον*) demonstration, and generalizes the interior angle-sum to any convex $n$-gon as $(2n - 4)$ right angles (*Friedlein* 383).
