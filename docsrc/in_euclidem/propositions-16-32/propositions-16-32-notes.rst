Commentary on Propositions I.16–I.32: Inequalities, Parallels & Angle-Sum — GEOMETOR Commentary & Concordance Notes
===================================================================================================================

GEOMETOR Philosophical & Mathematical Commentary
------------------------------------------------

1. **Neutral vs. Euclidean Geometry (`I.16`–`I.28` vs. `I.29`–`I.32`)**:
   Proclus's commentary makes visible the exact deductive fault line of Book I: every proposition through :just:`Prop. I.28 <I.28>` belongs to **neutral (absolute) geometry**, independent of :just:`Postulate 5 <I.post.5>` (provided straight lines are unbounded, as assumed in :just:`Prop. I.16 <I.16>`). Note Taylor's two 1792 typographic heading misprints in this span—`PROPOSITION XXIV` for `XXI` and `PROPOSITION XV` for `XXVI`—silently corrected in our English section headings.
2. **Ptolemy's Fallacy & Proclus's Equivalent Axiom (`Friedlein 365–373`)**:
   Examined in detail by both :cite:`Heath <heath:1926>` (Vol. I, pp. 204–207) and :cite:`Hoenen <hoenen:1954>` (Caput I, §5, p. 15), `Friedlein 365–373` is the foundational document of the history of non-Euclidean geometry:
   - :person:`Ptolemy <ptolemy>` argued (`365, 14–367, 27`) that interior angles on one side of a transversal are no more "interior" than those on the other side; Proclus (`368–370`) rightly exposes that symmetry applies to *either-side* pairs, not asymmetrically when one side sums to $< 2R$ and the other to $> 2R$.
   - Proclus's own proof (`371–373`) assumes that if two straight lines do not meet, the perpendicular distance between them remains bounded, whereas two rays diverging from a point exceed every finite distance (:cite:`Aristotle, De Caelo I.5 <aristotle:de-caelo:I.5>`). In hyperbolic geometry, two non-intersecting (ultraparallel/divergent) straight lines *also* diverge beyond every finite bound—proving that Proclus's bounded-distance premise is strictly equivalent to Euclid's Fifth Postulate!
3. **The Pythagorean Angle-Sum Proof (`Friedlein 379, 2–15`)**:
   Preserved from :person:`Eudemus <eudemus>`, the Pythagorean proof of :just:`Prop. I.32 <I.32>` draws a single line $DE \parallel BC$ through vertex $A$, immediately exhibiting the three angles of $\triangle ABC$ assembled into a straight angle ($2R$) around $A$ via alternate interior angles (:just:`I.29 <I.29>`) and :just:`Prop. I.13 <I.13>`.
