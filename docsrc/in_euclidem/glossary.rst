Greek & Neoplatonic Mathematical Glossary
=========================================

Lexicon of Greek mathematical, logical, and Neoplatonic epistemological terminology in Proclus's *Commentary on Book I*, cross-referenced with Euclid's *Elements* and Petrus Hoenen's Scholastic-noetic vocabulary.

.. _term-adynaton:

**apagoge eis adynaton** (*ἀπαγωγὴ εἰς ἀδύνατον*) · Lat. *reductio ad absurdum* — reduction to the impossible
  Indirect proof assuming the contradictory of the sought conclusion to deduce the overthrow of a conceded axiom, hypothesis, or prior theorem.
  *Friedlein loci*: 254–256.

.. _term-aitema:

**aitema** (*αἴτημα*) · Lat. *postulatum / petitio* — postulate
  A constructive geometric demand (Posts. 1–5) whose subject-matter is easy to contrive or grant without prior demonstration.
  *Friedlein loci*: 178–193.

.. _term-analysis:

**analysis** (*ἀνάλυσις*) · Lat. *analysis* — geometric analysis
  Upward path assuming the thing sought as granted and tracing consequences back to an admitted principle; transmitted by Plato to Leodamas.
  *Friedlein loci*: 43, 66–67, 211, 255.

.. _term-anamnesis:

**anamnesis** (*ἀνάμνησις*) · Lat. *reminiscentia* — recollection
  Platonic awakening of the soul's substantial mathematical logoi from within, illustrated by the Meno's doubling of the square.
  *Friedlein loci*: 44–46.

.. _term-antistrophe:

**antistrophe** (*ἀντιστροφή*) · Lat. *conversio* — geometric conversion
  Interchanging hypothesis and conclusion between leading (proegoumenon) and converse (antistrophon) theorems, either completely or partially.
  *Friedlein loci*: 252–254, 407.

.. _term-apagoge:

**apagoge** (*ἀπαγωγή*) · Lat. *reductio* — geometric reduction
  Transition from a harder problem or theorem to another whose solution immediately yields the proposed goal (e.g. Hippocrates on duplicating the cube).
  *Friedlein loci*: 212–213.

.. _term-apeiron:

**apeiron** (*ἄπειρον*) · Lat. *infinitum* — the Unlimited / Indefinite
  Complementary cosmic principle supplying inexhaustible progression, continuous divisibility, multiplicity, and infinite extendibility in magnitude and number.
  *Friedlein loci*: 5–7, 185, 278.

.. _term-apodeixis:

**apodeixis** (*ἀπόδειξις*) · Lat. *demonstratio* — scientific demonstration (5th of 6 parts)
  Syllogistic deduction inferring the sought attribute or construction validity from first principles through essential causes (aitiai).
  *Friedlein loci*: 203–207.

.. _term-axioma:

**axioma / koine ennoia** (*ἀξίωμα / κοινὴ ἔννοια*) · Lat. *axioma / communis animi conceptio* — axiom / common notion
  An indemonstrable, self-evident truth common to multiple sciences (e.g. 'things equal to the same are equal to one another').
  *Friedlein loci*: 76, 178–184, 193–198.

.. _term-dianoia:

**dianoia** (*διάνοια*) · Lat. *ratio discursiva* — discursive understanding
  The specifically mathematical faculty of the soul, unfolding innate intelligible logoi step-by-step from principles to conclusions.
  *Friedlein loci*: 3–4, 10–18, 51–57.

.. _term-diorismos:

**diorismos** (*διορισμός*) · Lat. *determinatio* — specification / condition of possibility (3rd of 6 parts)
  In theorems/problems, restating the sought goal on the set-out figure; in problems like I.22, also the necessary and sufficient condition for solvability.
  *Friedlein loci*: 66–67, 203–205, 330–331.

.. _term-doxa:

**doxa** (*δόξα*) · Lat. *opinio* — opinion
  Cognitive faculty directed toward mutable sensible things, lacking knowledge of Demonstrative Causes (aitiai).
  *Friedlein loci*: 3–4, 11.

.. _term-ekthesis:

**ekthesis** (*ἔκθεσις*) · Lat. *expositio* — setting-out (2nd of 6 parts)
  Instantiating the given of the enunciation in a concrete lettered figure in phantasia ('Let AB be the given finite straight line...').
  *Friedlein loci*: 203–207.

.. _term-elleipsis:

**elleipsis** (*ἔλλειψις*) · Lat. *defectus* — falling-short in application of areas
  Applying an area along a line so as to fall short of its extremity by a similar figure; origin of the name of the ellipse.
  *Friedlein loci*: 419–420.

.. _term-enstasis:

**enstasis** (*ἔνστασις*) · Lat. *instantia* — logical objection
  A counter-argument blocking either a geometric construction or a step of demonstration (e.g. Zeno of Sidon against I.1).
  *Friedlein loci*: 212–213, 214.

.. _term-epharmoge:

**epharmoge** (*ἐφαρμογή*) · Lat. *superpositio / congruentia* — superposition / coincidence
  Fitting one geometric magnitude onto another in imagination without physical distortion (CN 4, Prop. I.4, I.8).
  *Friedlein loci*: 188, 196, 234–240.

.. _term-gnomon:

**kata gnomona** (*κατὰ γνώμονα*) · Lat. *ad gnomonem / perpendiculariter* — gnomon-wise (perpendicular)
  Oenopides of Chios's archaic astronomical name for the perpendicular (Prop. I.12), drawn from the upright sundial pointer.
  *Friedlein loci*: 283.

.. _term-homoiomeres:

**homoiomeres** (*ὁμοιομερής*) · Lat. *uniformis / homoeomeris* — homoeomeric / uniform in all parts
  A curve every part of which coincides with every other part: proved by Geminus to comprise solely the straight line, circle, and cylindrical helix.
  *Friedlein loci*: 105, 112.

.. _term-horos:

**horos** (*ὅρος*) · Lat. *definitio / terminus* — definition / boundary
  Both a conceptual definition delimiting an essence (Defs. 1–23) and the geometric boundary enclosing a figure (Def. 13).
  *Friedlein loci*: 76, 85, 136–137.

.. _term-hyle-noete:

**hyle noete** (*ὕλη νοητή*) · Lat. *materia intelligibilis* — intelligible / imaginative matter
  Aristotelian term reinterpreted by Proclus as the pure spatial extension in phantasia individualizing plural congruent circles and triangles; central to Hoenen's Caput IV.
  *Friedlein loci*: 48–54.

.. _term-hyperbole:

**hyperbole** (*ὑπερβολή*) · Lat. *excessus* — exceeding in application of areas
  Applying an area along a line so as to overlap beyond its extremity by a similar figure; origin of the name of the hyperbola.
  *Friedlein loci*: 419–420.

.. _term-hypothesis:

**hypothesis** (*ὑπόθεσις*) · Lat. *hypothesis / suppositio* — hypothesis
  An unproved assumption posited by the teacher and conceded by the learner as the starting point of demonstration.
  *Friedlein loci*: 75–77, 178–184.

.. _term-hysterogenes:

**hysterogenes** (*ὑστερογενής*) · Lat. *posterius genitus* — later-born / abstracted universal
  Concept formed empirically a posteriori from sense-objects; rejected by Proclus for mathematics because sensibles lack exactness and necessity.
  *Friedlein loci*: 12–15, 50.

.. _term-kataskeue:

**kataskeue** (*κατασκευή*) · Lat. *constructio* — machinery / construction (4th of 6 parts)
  Auxiliary lines and circles drawn via postulates and earlier problems to prepare the figure for demonstration.
  *Friedlein loci*: 203–207.

.. _term-keratoeides:

**keratoeides gonia** (*κερατοειδὴς γωνία*) · Lat. *angulus cornicularis* — horn angle
  Curvilinear-rectilinear angle between a circle and its tangent, smaller than every acute rectilinear angle yet non-zero—challenging Archimedean magnitude.
  *Friedlein loci*: 121–127.

.. _term-lemma:

**lemma** (*λῆμμα*) · Lat. *lemma* — auxiliary proposition
  A proposition assumed in a proof while requiring independent confirmation elsewhere.
  *Friedlein loci*: 73, 211.

.. _term-metaxy:

**metaxy / mesotes** (*μεταξύ / μεσότης*) · Lat. *medietas* — intermediate status
  The middle rank occupied by mathematical reality and dianoia between pure Intellect (nous) and sense-perception/opinion (aisthesis/doxa).
  *Friedlein loci*: 3–5, 10–11.

.. _term-nous:

**nous** (*νοῦς*) · Lat. *intellectus* — Intellect
  Supreme intuitive intelligence contemplating simple, partless, unextended archetypal Forms prior to discursive unfolding.
  *Friedlein loci*: 3–4, 10–18.

.. _term-ousia-mathematike:

**ousia mathematike** (*οὐσία μαθηματική*) · Lat. *essentia mathematica* — mathematical essence / being
  The intermediate ontological realm of mathematical objects: unmoved and exact unlike sensibles, yet pluralized and extended unlike pure indivisible Forms.
  *Friedlein loci*: 3–5.

.. _term-parabole:

**parabole** (*παραβολή*) · Lat. *applicatio spatiorum* — application of areas (exact)
  Pythagorean construction applying a given area exactly along a given segment (Prop. I.44); transferred by Apollonius to the parabola.
  *Friedlein loci*: 419–420.

.. _term-peras:

**peras** (*πέρας*) · Lat. *terminus / finis* — Limit
  First cosmic principle (from Plato's Philebus) bestowing boundary, definite ratio, symmetry, and intelligible form upon mathematical figures; also geometric extremity.
  *Friedlein loci*: 5–7, 136.

.. _term-phantasia:

**phantasia** (*φαντασία*) · Lat. *imaginatio / phantasia* — mathematical imagination
  Formative intellective-imaginative screen ('passive nous') onto which dianoia projects universal logoi as extended, divisible, yet exact geometric figures.
  *Friedlein loci*: 51–57, 78–79, 141.

.. _term-porisma:

**porisma** (*πόρισμα*) · Lat. *porisma / corollarium* — porism / corollary
  Either (1) an incidental gain emerging unbidden from a demonstration (as I.15 Porism), or (2) finding an existing geometric entity intermediate between problem and theorem.
  *Friedlein loci*: 212, 301–305.

.. _term-problema:

**problema** (*πρόβλημα*) · Lat. *problema* — problem
  A proposition proposing the constructive generation, division, or placement of a geometric figure not yet brought to view (ending 'what it was required to do').
  *Friedlein loci*: 77–81, 200–202, 241–243.

.. _term-probole:

**probole** (*προβολή*) · Lat. *proiectio* — projection
  The active projection by which dianoia unfolds its partless internal reason-principles onto the mirror-like screen of phantasia to examine their properties.
  *Friedlein loci*: 51–56, 78–79, 141.

.. _term-protasis:

**protasis** (*πρότασις*) · Lat. *propositio* — enunciation (1st of 6 parts)
  Universal statement declaring what is given and what is sought, prior to any lettered diagram.
  *Friedlein loci*: 203–204.

.. _term-ptosis:

**ptosis** (*πτῶσις*) · Lat. *casus* — geometric case
  Positional variation of a construction (e.g. point inside, on, or outside a figure) requiring distinct verification of the same theorem.
  *Friedlein loci*: 212, 221.

.. _term-rhysis:

**rhysis** (*ῥύσις*) · Lat. *fluxus* — generative flux
  Unextended motion of a point generating a line, or uniform undeviating flux generating a straight line (cited in Greek by Hoenen at p. 121).
  *Friedlein loci*: 97, 100, 185.

.. _term-stoicheion:

**stoicheion** (*στοιχεῖον*) · Lat. *elementum* — element
  A foundational proposition whose mastery pervades and unlocks the demonstration of many subsequent theorems, as letters compose speech.
  *Friedlein loci*: 71–74.

.. _term-stoicheiosis:

**stoicheiosis** (*στοιχείωσις*) · Lat. *elementatio* — elementary treatise / axiomatic system
  Systematic deductive arrangement ascending from first principles through elementary propositions to the capstone of a science.
  *Friedlein loci*: 70–75, 84, 432.

.. _term-symperasma:

**symperasma** (*συμπέρασμα*) · Lat. *conclusio* — conclusion (6th of 6 parts)
  Re-ascending from the particular lettered diagram back to the universal truth of the enunciation, sealed by Q.E.F. or Q.E.D.
  *Friedlein loci*: 203–207.

.. _term-synthesis:

**synthesis** (*σύνθεσις*) · Lat. *synthesis* — geometric synthesis
  Downward deductive path proceeding from admitted principles through intermediate steps to the conclusion.
  *Friedlein loci*: 43, 211, 255.

.. _term-theorema:

**theorema** (*θεώρημα*) · Lat. *theorema* — theorem
  A proposition demonstrating an essential attribute belonging per se to a given geometric figure (ending 'what it was required to prove').
  *Friedlein loci*: 77–81, 200–202, 241–243.

.. _term-topikon-theorema:

**topikon theorema** (*τοπικὸν θεώρημα*) · Lat. *theorema locale* — locus theorem
  Theorem invariant across an infinite locus of positions—inaugurated in the Elements by Prop. I.35 (equal parallelograms between parallel lines).
  *Friedlein loci*: 394–396.
