Def. 3: Extremities of a Line & Homoeomeric Lines — GEOMETOR Commentary & Concordance Notes
===========================================================================================

GEOMETOR Philosophical & Mathematical Commentary
------------------------------------------------

Proclus analyzes how points bound a line (:just:`I.def.3`) and preserves :person:`Geminus's <geminus>` classification of curves—proving (*Friedlein* 105) that only three curves are :term:`homoeomeric <homoiomeres>` (straight line, circle, cylindrical helix).

Friedlein Loci Cited in GEOMETOR (3 Loci in pp. 103–109)
--------------------------------------------------------

* :ref:`Friedlein 103, 21–107, 10 <conc-friedlein-103-21-107-10>` (:ref:`text <sec-def-03>`) — Classification of lines; Geminus and Apollonius on homoeomeric lines (straight line, circle, cylindrical helix).
  - ``euclid`` (``proclus:in-euclid:103.21-107.10``): Heath Vol. I, p. 40 [folio ``0054``]
* :ref:`Friedlein 105, 5 <conc-friedlein-105-5>` (:ref:`text <sec-def-03>`) — Proof from Geminus that only three lines are homoeomeric (alike in all parts): the straight line, the circle, and the cylindrical helix.
  - ``euclid`` (``proclus:in-euclid:105.5``): Heath Vol. I, p. 162 [folio ``0176``]
* :ref:`Friedlein 105, 11 <conc-friedlein-105-11>` (:ref:`text <sec-def-03>`) — Why the cylindrical helix, though homoeomeric, is composite rather than simple because it is generated by two dissimilar motions.
  - ``euclid`` (``proclus:in-euclid:105.11``): Heath Vol. I, p. 162 [folio ``0176``]
