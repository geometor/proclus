Def. 4: Straight Line (Eutheia) & Spiric/Conic Classifications — GEOMETOR Commentary & Concordance Notes
========================================================================================================

GEOMETOR Philosophical & Mathematical Commentary
------------------------------------------------

Proclus harmonizes :just:`I.def.4` (*"lies evenly with the points on itself"*) with :person:`Plato's <plato>` optical definition (:cite:`Parmenides 137e <plato:parmenides:137e>`) and :person:`Archimedes' <archimedes>` shortest-distance axiom (*Friedlein* 109–110), and records :person:`Perseus's <perseus>` spiric sections (*Friedlein* 111–112).

Friedlein Loci Cited in GEOMETOR (3 Loci in pp. 109–113)
--------------------------------------------------------

* :ref:`Friedlein 110, 18–23 <conc-friedlein-110-18-23>` (:ref:`text <sec-def-04>`) — Famous alternative definitions of a straight line: stretched to the utmost, Plato's 'middle covers the ends' (Parmenides 137E), and Archimedes' shortest path.
  - ``euclid`` (``proclus:in-euclid:110.18-23``): Heath Vol. I, p. 168 [folio ``0182``]
* :ref:`Friedlein 111, 9–20 <conc-friedlein-111-9-20>` (:ref:`text <sec-def-04>`) — Geminus's systematic classification of lines into simple (circular, straight, indefinite) and composite/mixed (in planes vs. on solids).
  - ``euclid`` (``proclus:in-euclid:111.9-20``): Heath Vol. I, p. 161 [folio ``0175``]
* :ref:`Friedlein 112, 1 <conc-friedlein-112-1>` (:ref:`text <sec-def-04>`) — Perseus's discovery of the three spiric curves (sections of the torus/spira) and quotation of Perseus's dedicatory epigram.
  - ``euclid`` (``proclus:in-euclid:112.1``): Heath Vol. I, p. 162 [folio ``0176``]
