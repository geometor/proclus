Commentary on the Definitions (I.Def.1–23) — Analytical Summary
===============================================================

*Scope*: **Friedlein pp. 85–177** · *Book II (Definitions I–XXXV)*

Architectural Argument of the Definitions Commentary
----------------------------------------------------

Across ninety-three pages (*Friedlein* pp. 85–177; Taylor Book II, Definitions I–XXXV), :person:`Proclus <proclus>` expounds :person:`Euclid's <euclid>` twenty-three Book I Definitions (:just:`I.Def.1 <I.def.1>`–:just:`I.Def.23 <I.def.23>`) as a systematic descent from indivisible unity into planar figure:

1. **Point, Line, and Straight Line (*Friedlein* 85–113)**:
   - :just:`Def. 1 <I.def.1>` (*Point*): Proclus contrasts Euclid's negative definition ("that which has no part") with the Pythagorean positive definition ("a monad having position", *Friedlein* 95, 21), showing why negative characterization befits the first limit of magnitude.
   - :just:`Def. 2–3 <I.def.2>` (*Line & Extremities*): Alongside "breadthless length", Proclus examines the dynamic generation of the line as the :term:`flux of a point <rhysis>` (*Friedlein* 97, 6–13) and preserves :person:`Geminus's <geminus>` classification of curves—proving (*Friedlein* 105) that only three curves are :term:`homoeomeric <homoiomeres>` (straight line, circle, cylindrical helix) and recording :person:`Perseus's <perseus>` spiric sections (*Friedlein* 111–112).
   - :just:`Def. 4 <I.def.4>` (*Straight Line*): Harmonizes Euclid's enigmatic *"lies evenly with the points on itself"* with :person:`Plato's <plato>` optical definition (:cite:`Parmenides 137e <plato:parmenides:137e>`) and :person:`Archimedes' <archimedes>` shortest-distance axiom (*Friedlein* 109–110).
2. **Surface, Plane, and the Ontology of Angles (*Friedlein* 113–136)**:
   - :just:`Defs. 5–7 <I.def.7>` (*Surface & Plane*): Traces planar flatness in parallel with rectilinear evenness.
   - :just:`Defs. 8–12 <I.def.8>` (*Plane Angle, Right, Obtuse & Acute*): Preserves the celebrated debate of :person:`Eudemus <eudemus>`, :person:`Carpus of Antioch <carpus>`, :person:`Plutarch of Athens <plutarch-of-athens>`, and :person:`Syrianus <syrianus>` over whether an angle belongs to Aristotle's category of *Relation*, *Quality*, or *Quantity*. The decisive test-case is the :term:`horn angle <keratoeides>` (*Friedlein* 121–127): divisible yet non-Archimedean relative to rectilinear angles! In :just:`Defs. 10–12 <I.def.10>`, the right angle embodies :term:`Limit <peras>` and equality, whereas obtuse and acute embody :term:`the Unlimited <apeiron>`.
3. **Figure, Circle, Polygons, and Parallels (*Friedlein* 136–177)**:
   - :just:`Defs. 13–14 <I.def.14>` (*Boundary & Figure*): Refutes :person:`Protagoras's <protagoras>` empiricist attack on circle-line tangency (*Friedlein* 139–140) and contrasts Euclid's contained-space figure with :person:`Posidonius's <posidonius>` enclosing-perimeter figure (*Friedlein* 143).
   - :just:`Defs. 15–18 <I.def.15>` (*Circle & Diameter*): Celebrates the circle as first and fairest of plane figures and records :person:`Thales's <thales>` first proof that the diameter bisects the circle (*Friedlein* 157, 10).
   - :just:`Defs. 19–22 <I.def.20>` (*Triangles & Quadrilaterals*): Proves combinatorially why there are **seven** (not nine) side-angle species of triangles (*Friedlein* 165, 24) and organizes Posidonius's sevenfold taxonomy of quadrilaterals (*Friedlein* 169–174).
   - :just:`Def. 23 <I.def.23>` (*Parallel Lines*): Contrasts Euclid's coplanar non-intersection definition with Posidonius's equidistance definition, preserving :person:`Geminus's <geminus>` profound warning on asymptotic curves (*Friedlein* 176–177).
