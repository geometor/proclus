Textual Transmission & Critical Editions
========================================

Manuscript Tradition
--------------------

The principal Greek codices of Proclus's *Commentary on Book I* descend from Byzantine scholarly archetypes, led by **Codex Monacensis Graecus 427** (*M*, 11th/12th century, Munich, Bayerische Staatsbibliothek), alongside Vatican, Laurentian, Bologna, Marcian, and Bodleian witnesses. All extant Greek manuscripts share small mechanical lacunae—most famously at the junction of Propositions I.36 and I.37 (*Friedlein* 403, 5).

Key Printed Editions & Translations
-----------------------------------

1. **Simon Grynaeus — Basel Editio Princeps (1533)**:
   Appended to Johann Herwagen's *editio princeps* of the Greek *Elements* (*Εὐκλείδου Στοιχείων βιβλ. ιε΄*, Basel, 1533), Grynaeus printed Proclus's Greek commentary from an imperfect Oxford manuscript containing numerous omissions and corruptions. Grynaeus's pagination is preserved in the outer margin of Friedlein (`G.`).
2. **Franciscus Barocius — Padua Latin Translation (1560)**:
   The Venetian patrician and mathematician **Francesco Barozzi** (*Barocius*) collated six Greek manuscripts in Crete, Venice, and Bologna to publish his landmark Latin translation (*Padua, 1560*), restoring major passages missing from the 1533 Basel edition and supplying clear geometric diagrams (`B.` pagination in Friedlein).
3. **Thomas Taylor — London English Translation (1788–1789 / 1792)**:
   Working from Grynaeus's Greek text supplemented continuously by Barocius's Latin edition, the English Platonist **Thomas Taylor** published the first translation into any modern vernacular in two quarto volumes (*London, 1788–1789*, reissued *1792*; Project Gutenberg `#74253` and `#79455`).
4. **Gottfried Friedlein — Leipzig Teubner Critical Greek Edition (1873)**:
   Collating *Monacensis 427* (*M*) and its sister witnesses directly against Grynaeus and Barocius, **Gottfried Friedlein** established the authoritative critical Greek text (*Leipzig: B. G. Teubner, 1873*, pp. 1–436, *Indices* pp. 437–507; CTS `urn:cts:greekLit:tlg4036.tlg011.opp-grc1`), whose page-and-line numbers form the universal scholarly standard across Sir Thomas Heath (`geometor/euclid`), Petrus Hoenen (`geometor/hoenen`), and this Codex.
