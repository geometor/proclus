Commentary on the Definitions (I.Def.1–23)

Across ninety-three pages (Friedlein pp. 85–177; Taylor Book II, Definitions I–XXXV), Proclus expounds Euclid's twenty-three Book I Definitions ([I.def.1]–[I.def.23]) as a systematic descent from indivisible unity into planar figure. Each section below presents four synchronized tracks: English, Greek (1873), Summary, and Notes.

Hypotheses, Postulates, Axioms & Propositional Form

Bridging Book I’s Definitions and its forty-eight Propositions (Friedlein pp. 178–207; Taylor Book III opening), Proclus establishes both the epistemology of unproved geometric principles and the formal logic of Euclidean demonstration. Each section below presents four synchronized tracks: English, Greek (1873), Summary, and Notes.

Commentary on the Propositions of Book I

Comprising Friedlein pp. 208–436 (Books III & IV of Taylor’s division), Proclus’s commentary on the forty-eight propositions of Euclid’s First Book forms the systematic climax of the treatise. Here Proclus scrutinizes every problem and theorem—its canonical six parts (proposition, exposition, determination, construction, demonstration, conclusion), its alternative constructions and cases (ptosis), objections (enstasis), lemmata, and corollaries (porisms).
