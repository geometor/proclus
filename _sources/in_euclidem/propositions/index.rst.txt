Commentary on the Propositions of Book I
========================================

Comprising **Friedlein pp. 208–436** (*Books III & IV* of Taylor's division), Proclus's commentary on the forty-eight propositions of Euclid's First Book forms the systematic climax of the treatise. Here Proclus scrutinizes every problem and theorem—its canonical six parts (proposition, exposition, determination, construction, demonstration, conclusion), its alternative constructions and cases (:term:`ptosis <ptosis>`), objections (:term:`enstasis <enstasis>`), lemmata, and corollaries (:term:`porisms <porisma>`).

Architectural Structure of Book I
---------------------------------

### Triangles, Congruence & Perpendiculars (Props. I.1–I.15, *Friedlein* 208–305)

In the first fifteen propositions of Book I (*Friedlein* pp. 208–305), :person:`Euclid <euclid>` establishes the elementary constructive toolkit, fundamental triangle congruence laws, bisections, perpendiculars, and linear angle relations—all without ever invoking parallel lines:

1. **Constructive Foundations & Zeno's Critique (:just:`I.1`–:just:`I.3`, *Friedlein* 208–232)**:
   - :just:`Prop. I.1 <I.1>` opens the *Elements* by constructing the equilateral triangle, the most symmetric rectilinear archetype, and serves as Proclus's live laboratory for the Six Canonical Parts, :term:`lemma <lemma>`, :term:`case <ptosis>`, :term:`porism <porisma>`, :term:`objection <enstasis>`, and :term:`reduction <apagoge>` (*Friedlein* 208–213). Proclus also preserves :person:`Zeno of Sidon's <zeno-of-sidon>` incisive Epicurean objection—that two straight lines might share a common segment—together with :person:`Posidonius's <posidonius>` reply (*Friedlein* 214–218).
   - :just:`Props. I.2–I.3 <I.2>` emancipate the compass from collapsing by transferring and subtracting segments across arbitrary positions; Proclus enumerates the positional cases omitted by Euclid.
2. **First Congruence, Isosceles Symmetry & Conversion Theory (:just:`I.4`–:just:`I.8`, *Friedlein* 233–269)**:
   - :just:`Prop. I.4 <I.4>` (SAS) is the first *theorem* in Euclid, proved via :term:`superposition <epharmoge>`; Proclus appends the debate between :person:`Carpus of Antioch <carpus>` and :person:`Geminus <geminus>` on whether problems precede theorems (*Friedlein* 241–243).
   - :just:`Prop. I.5 <I.5>` (*pons asinorum*) records :person:`Thales's <thales>` discovery alongside :person:`Pappus's <pappus>` astonishing zero-construction proof (*Friedlein* 249–250), comparing :math:`\triangle ABC` directly with its mirror orientation :math:`\triangle ACB`!
   - :just:`Prop. I.6 <I.6>` occasions Proclus's master-analysis of :term:`geometric conversion <antistrophe>` (Menaechmus and Amphinomus) and :term:`reductio ad absurdum <adynaton>` (*Friedlein* 252–256).
   - :just:`Props. I.7–I.8 <I.8>` establish SSS congruence, enriched with :person:`Philo of Byzantium's <philo>` elegant three-case proof bypassing Lemma I.7 (*Friedlein* 266–268).
3. **Bisections, Perpendiculars, Vertical Angles & Porisms (:just:`I.9`–:just:`I.15`, *Friedlein* 270–305)**:
   - :just:`Props. I.9–I.10 <I.9>` bisect any rectilinear angle and finite segment—explaining why **angle trisection** requires higher curves (conchoids of :person:`Nicomedes <nicomedes>`, quadratrices of :person:`Hippias <hippias>`, spirals of :person:`Archimedes <archimedes>`, *Friedlein* 272) and why universal bisectability refutes :person:`Xenocrates' <xenocrates>` *indivisible lines* (*Friedlein* 277–279).
   - :just:`Props. I.11–I.12 <I.12>` erect and drop perpendiculars, recording :person:`Apollonius's <apollonius>` constructions and :person:`Oenopides of Chios's <oenopides>` astronomical term :term:`kata gnomona <gnomon>` (*Friedlein* 283).
   - :just:`Props. I.13–I.15 <I.15>` establish supplementary and vertical angles (discovered by :person:`Thales <thales>`, proved scientifically by Euclid, *Friedlein* 299) and conclude with Proclus's famous **Excursus on Porisms** (*Friedlein* 301–305).

### Inequalities, Parallels & Angle-Sum (Props. I.16–I.32, *Friedlein* 305–384)

Spanning the second half of Proclus's Book III and the dramatic opening of Book IV (*Friedlein* pp. 305–384), **Propositions I.16–I.32** carry Euclidean geometry from **absolute (neutral) triangle inequalities** across the **watershed of the Parallel Postulate** to the **universal interior angle-sum of the triangle**:

1. **Neutral Geometry of Inequalities & Triangle Determination (:just:`I.16`–:just:`I.26`, *Friedlein* 305–353)**:
   - :just:`Props. I.16–I.17 <I.16>` prove without Postulate 5 that an exterior angle exceeds either remote interior angle and that any two angles of a triangle are less than two right angles.
   - :just:`Props. I.18–I.21 <I.20>` pair greater sides with greater angles and prove the **Triangle Inequality** (:just:`I.20 <I.20>`). When Epicureans mock I.20 as *"evident even to an ass walking straight to its hay"* (*Friedlein* 322), Proclus replies that sense perceives only *that* (*τὸ ὅτι*), whereas science demands *why* (*τὸ διότι*), and preserves :person:`Heron's <heron>` and :person:`Porphyry's <porphyry>` alternative proofs (*Friedlein* 323–326).
   - :just:`Props. I.22–I.23 <I.22>` construct triangles from three given segments—vindicating the necessity of the :term:`diorismos <diorismos>`—and copy any rectilinear angle (attributed by :person:`Eudemus <eudemus>` to :person:`Oenopides <oenopides>`, *Friedlein* 333).
   - :just:`Props. I.24–I.25 <I.24>` establish the Hinge Theorems (enriched with :person:`Menelaus's <menelaus>` direct proof, *Friedlein* 345–347).
   - :just:`Prop. I.26 <I.26>` completes triangle congruence (ASA and AAS), featuring Porphyry's exhaustive combinatorial proof of why SSA and AAA fail (*Friedlein* 351) and Eudemus's report that :person:`Thales <thales>` used I.26 to determine **the distance of ships at sea** (*Friedlein* 352, 14–18).
2. **Theory of Parallels & The Attempted Proofs of Postulate 5 (:just:`I.27`–:just:`I.31`, *Friedlein* 354–376)**:
   - :just:`Props. I.27–I.28 <I.27>` establish sufficient conditions for parallel lines without Postulate 5.
   - At :just:`Prop. I.29 <I.29>`—the exact moment Euclid first invokes :just:`Postulate 5 <I.post.5>`—Proclus inserts his monumental nine-page critical excursion (*Friedlein* 365–373): (a) summarizing :person:`Claudius Ptolemy's <ptolemy>` lost treatise attempting to prove I.29 and Postulate 5; (b) exposing the hidden symmetry fallacy in Ptolemy's argument; (c) refuting a Zeno-like infinite-bisection sophism; and (d) presenting **Proclus's own attempted proof**, explicitly assuming :person:`Aristotle's <aristotle>` lemma that diverging rays separate beyond any finite bound—thereby isolating bounded equidistance as an exact logical equivalent of the Parallel Postulate!
3. **The Triangle Angle-Sum & Pythagorean Demonstration (:just:`I.32`, *Friedlein* 376–384)**:
   - :just:`Prop. I.32 <I.32>` proves that the exterior angle equals the two remote interiors and the three interior angles equal two right angles. Proclus preserves **the ancient Pythagorean proof** via :person:`Eudemus <eudemus>` (*Friedlein* 379, 2–15), analyzes :person:`Aristotle's <aristotle>` doctrine of commensurately universal (*καθόλου πρῶτον*) demonstration, and generalizes the interior angle-sum to any convex :math:`n`-gon as :math:`(2n - 4)` right angles (*Friedlein* 383).

### Areas, Application & Pythagoras (Props. I.33–I.48, *Friedlein* 384–436)

In the grand finale of Book I (*Friedlein* pp. 384–432), :person:`Euclid <euclid>` ascends from congruence to **equivalence of area**, culminating in the **Pythagorean Application of Areas** and **the Theorem of Pythagoras**:

1. **Parallelograms & The Birth of Locus Theorems (:just:`I.33`–:just:`I.41`, *Friedlein* 384–415)**:
   - :just:`Props. I.33–I.34 <I.34>` bridge parallel lines and quadrilateral figures, generating the *parallelogrammic area* (*παραλληλόγραμμον χωρίον*) and proving its bisection by the diameter.
   - :just:`Prop. I.35 <I.35>` (*parallelograms on the same base and in the same parallels are equal*) inaugurates a new level of geometric equality—*equality of area without congruence of form*—and is hailed by Proclus as the first **locus theorem** (:term:`τοπικὸν θεώρημα <topikon-theorema>`, *Friedlein* 394–396), since the upper side may slide infinitely along the parallel locus while preserving constant area even as perimeter grows without bound!
   - :just:`Props. I.36–I.41 <I.41>` extend area invariance to parallelograms and triangles on equal bases, examine partial converses (:just:`I.39`–:just:`I.40`), and establish that a parallelogram is double a triangle sharing its base and parallels (:just:`I.41`), to which Proclus adds an original exercise on the area of trapezia (*Friedlein* 414–415).
2. **Complements & The Pythagorean Application of Areas (:just:`I.42`–:just:`I.45`, *Friedlein* 415–423)**:
   - :just:`Prop. I.43 <I.43>` proves the equality of the **complements** (*παραπληρώματα*) about the diameter of any parallelogram—the algebraic engine of Greek geometric transformation.
   - On :just:`Prop. I.44 <I.44>`, Proclus preserves one of the jewels of the history of mathematics (*Friedlein* 419–420): :person:`Eudemus's <eudemus>` testimony that **application of areas** (:term:`παραβολή <parabole>`), **exceeding** (:term:`ὑπερβολή <hyperbole>`), and **falling-short** (:term:`ἔλλειψις <elleipsis>`) are *"ancient discoveries of the Muse of the Pythagoreans"*, providing the foundation for Book II geometric algebra, Book VI quadratic equations, and :person:`Apollonius's <apollonius>` naming of the three conic sections!
3. **The Square & The Theorem of Pythagoras (:just:`I.46`–:just:`I.48`, *Friedlein* 423–432)**:
   - :just:`Prop. I.46 <I.46>` constructs the square on any segment.
   - On :just:`Prop. I.47 <I.47>`, Proclus treats the anecdote of :person:`Pythagoras's <pythagoras>` ox-sacrifice with scholarly circumspection (*Friedlein* 426, 6–9), expressing supreme admiration for **Euclid himself** both for the luminous Book I area-shearing demonstration (via the famous "windmill" configuration of :just:`I.4`, :just:`I.41`, and :just:`I.46`) and for generalizing the theorem in Book VI (:just:`VI.31`) to arbitrary similar figures! He also records the two classical integer right-triangle generators (*Friedlein* 428–429): the **Pythagorean odd-number rule** and the **Platonic even-number rule**.
   - :just:`Prop. I.48 <I.48>` proves the converse of I.47 and closes the *Commentary* (*Friedlein* 432) with a tribute to :person:`Syrianus <syrianus>` and an invitation to future philosophical commentators on the remaining books of Euclid.

GEOMETOR Philosophical & Mathematical Commentary
------------------------------------------------

### Foundational Critiques & Symmetry (Props. I.1–I.15)

1. **Zeno of Sidon's Continuity & Uniqueness Critiques (`Friedlein 214–218`)**:
   In :just:`Prop. I.1 <I.1>`, two questions arise that modern axiomatics (Pasch, Hilbert) later formalized: (a) why must the two circles intersect? and (b) as :person:`Zeno of Sidon <zeno-of-sidon>` asked, how do we know the two radii :math:`CA, CB` do not share a common segment near :math:`C`? In `geometor/model`, exact polynomial intersection of the two unit circles :math:`(x)^2 + y^2 = 1` and :math:`(x - 1)^2 + y^2 = 1` algebraically exhibits the two conjugate equilateral vertices :math:`(1/2, \pm \sqrt{3}/2)` over :math:`\mathbb{Q}(\sqrt{3})`, while Zeno's uniqueness critique highlights the implicit uniqueness content of :just:`Postulates 1–2 <I.post.1>` defended by :person:`Posidonius <posidonius>`.
2. **Pappus's Zero-Construction Symmetry Proof of `I.5` (`Friedlein 249–250`)**:
   Preserved exclusively here (`proclus:in-euclid:249.20-250.12`), :person:`Pappus's <pappus>` proof of :just:`Prop. I.5 <I.5>` conceives the single isosceles triangle :math:`ABC` (:math:`AB = AC`) simultaneously as :math:`\triangle ABC` and :math:`\triangle ACB`, applying :just:`Prop. I.4 <I.4>` directly without a single auxiliary line—an early triumph of transformational/reflection symmetry in synthetic geometry.
3. **Trisection, Indivisible Lines & Oenopides' Gnomon (`Friedlein 272–289`)**:
   Proclus's distinction between straightedge-and-compass angle bisection (:just:`I.9 <I.9>`) and general angle trisection (`Friedlein 272`, requiring cubic/transcendental curves—conics, conchoids, or quadratrices) anticipates Wantzel's 1837 algebraic degree criterion (:math:`2^k` vs. degree :math:`3`), while :person:`Oenopides' <oenopides>` astronomical perpendicular (:just:`I.12 <I.12>`, `Friedlein 283, 7–10`) anchors Book I's constructive restrictions in classical celestial measurement.

### Neutral Geometry & The Deductive Fault Line (Props. I.16–I.32)

1. **Neutral vs. Euclidean Geometry (`I.16`–`I.28` vs. `I.29`–`I.32`)**:
   Proclus's commentary makes visible the exact deductive fault line of Book I: every proposition through :just:`Prop. I.28 <I.28>` belongs to **neutral (absolute) geometry**, independent of :just:`Postulate 5 <I.post.5>` (provided straight lines are unbounded, as assumed in :just:`Prop. I.16 <I.16>`). Note Taylor's two 1792 typographic heading misprints in this span—`PROPOSITION XXIV` for `XXI` and `PROPOSITION XV` for `XXVI`—silently corrected in our English section headings.
2. **Ptolemy's Fallacy & Proclus's Equivalent Axiom (`Friedlein 365–373`)**:
   Examined in detail by both :cite:`Heath <heath:1926>` (Vol. I, pp. 204–207) and :cite:`Hoenen <hoenen:1954>` (Caput I, §5, p. 15), `Friedlein 365–373` is the foundational document of the history of non-Euclidean geometry:
   - :person:`Ptolemy <ptolemy>` argued (`365, 14–367, 27`) that interior angles on one side of a transversal are no more "interior" than those on the other side; Proclus (`368–370`) rightly exposes that symmetry applies to *either-side* pairs, not asymmetrically when one side sums to :math:`< 2R` and the other to :math:`> 2R`.
   - Proclus's own proof (`371–373`) assumes that if two straight lines do not meet, the perpendicular distance between them remains bounded, whereas two rays diverging from a point exceed every finite distance (:cite:`Aristotle, De Caelo I.5 <aristotle:de-caelo:I.5>`). In hyperbolic geometry, two non-intersecting (ultraparallel/divergent) straight lines *also* diverge beyond every finite bound—proving that Proclus's bounded-distance premise is strictly equivalent to Euclid's Fifth Postulate!
3. **The Pythagorean Angle-Sum Proof (`Friedlein 379, 2–15`)**:
   Preserved from :person:`Eudemus <eudemus>`, the Pythagorean proof of :just:`Prop. I.32 <I.32>` draws a single line :math:`DE \parallel BC` through vertex :math:`A`, immediately exhibiting the three angles of :math:`\triangle ABC` assembled into a straight angle (:math:`2R`) around :math:`A` via alternate interior angles (:just:`I.29 <I.29>`) and :just:`Prop. I.13 <I.13>`.

### Locus Theorems, Area Application & Pythagoras (Props. I.33–I.48)

1. **Locus Theorems (`Friedlein 394–396`) & Textual Lacuna at `I.36`–`I.37` (`Friedlein 403, 5`)**:
   Proclus's recognition of :just:`Prop. I.35 <I.35>` as the prototype of **locus theorems** (:term:`τοπικὰ θεωρήματα <topikon-theorema>`) links elementary area-shearing to higher Greek locus geometry (:person:`Hermotimus <hermotimus>`, Aristaeus, :person:`Apollonius <apollonius>`). Note at `Friedlein 403, 5` (cited by :cite:`Heath <heath:1926>`, Vol. I, p. 332) a famous manuscript lacuna spanning the end of Proclus's note on :just:`I.36 <I.36>` and the opening of :just:`I.37 <I.37>`, present in all extant Greek codices and noted by :person:`Barocius <barocius>` and :person:`Friedlein <friedlein>`. (Also note Taylor's 1792 heading misprint `PROPOSITION LXVIII` for `XLVIII`, normalized in our heading.)
2. **Application of Areas (`Friedlein 419–420`) & The Golden Section**:
   Proclus's preservation of :person:`Eudemus's <eudemus>` report on :just:`Prop. I.44 <I.44>`—that :term:`parabole <parabole>`, :term:`hyperbole <hyperbole>`, and :term:`elleipsis <elleipsis>` belong to the ancient Pythagorean muse—unlocks the unity of Greek geometry:
   - Exact application (*parabole*, :just:`I.44 <I.44>`–:just:`I.45 <I.45>`) solves :math:`ax = S` geometrically via the gnomon complement (:just:`I.43 <I.43>`).
   - Application with square excess (*hyperbole*) in :just:`II.11 <II.11>` and :just:`VI.30 <VI.30>` (:math:`x(a + x) = a^2`) constructs **division in extreme and mean ratio**—the **Divine Proportion** :math:`\phi` at the heart of **GEOMETOR**!
3. **Euclid's Proof of `I.47` & Pythagorean Triples (`Friedlein 426–429`)**:
   Proclus is our sole ancient source crediting **Euclid personally** with the area-shearing Book I proof of :just:`Prop. I.47 <I.47>` (necessary because Book V's Eudoxian proportion theory has not yet been introduced in Book I!) as well as recording side-by-side the **Pythagorean odd generator** :math:`(2k+1,\; 2k^2+2k,\; 2k^2+2k+1)` and the **Platonic even generator** :math:`(2m,\; m^2-1,\; m^2+1)`.
