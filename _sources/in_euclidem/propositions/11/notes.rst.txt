Prop. I.11: Erecting a Perpendicular at a Point on a Line — GEOMETOR Commentary & Concordance Notes
===================================================================================================

GEOMETOR Philosophical & Mathematical Commentary
------------------------------------------------

Proclus's commentary on :just:`Prop. I.11 <I.11>` (*Friedlein* pp. 280–284) forms an essential link in Book I's systematic construction of plane geometry.

Friedlein Loci Cited in GEOMETOR (5 Loci in pp. 280–284)
-------------------------------------------------------------------------

* :ref:`Friedlein 279, 16–280, 4 <conc-friedlein-279-16-280-4>` (:ref:`text <sec-prop-11>`) — Apollonius of Perga's symmetric two-circle construction for bisecting a finite straight line (Prop. I.10) without first invoking Prop. I.9.
  - ``euclid`` (``proclus:in-euclid:279.16-280.4``): Heath Vol. I, p. 268 (Bk. I, propositions) [folio ``0282``]
* :ref:`Friedlein 280, 9 <conc-friedlein-280-9>` (:ref:`text <sec-prop-11>`) — Commentary on Prop. I.11: relation between bisecting a segment and erecting a perpendicular at a given point.
  - ``euclid`` (``proclus:in-euclid:280.9``): Heath Vol. I, p. 32 (chapter_iv) [folio ``0046``]
* :ref:`Friedlein 282, 8 <conc-friedlein-282-8>` (:ref:`text <sec-prop-11>`) — Apollonius's alternative construction for erecting a perpendicular to a straight line from a point on it (Prop. I.11).
  - ``euclid`` (``proclus:in-euclid:282.8``): Heath Vol. I, p. 270 (Bk. I, propositions) [folio ``0284``]
* :ref:`Friedlein 283, 7–10 <conc-friedlein-283-7-10>` (:ref:`text <sec-prop-11>`) — Commentary on Prop. I.12: Oenopides of Chios first investigated dropping a perpendicular for astronomy and called the perpendicular 'drawn gnomon-wise' (kata gnomona).
  - ``euclid`` (``proclus:in-euclid:283.7-10``): Heath Vol. I, p. 271 (Bk. I, propositions) [folio ``0285``]
* :ref:`Friedlein 283, 9 <conc-friedlein-283-9>` (:ref:`text <sec-prop-11>`) — Archaic Greek terminology of Oenopides: calling the perpendicular kata gnomona because the sundial gnomon stands at right angles to the horizon.
  - ``euclid`` (``proclus:in-euclid:283.9``): Heath Vol. I, p. 181 (Bk. I, first_principles) [folio ``0195``]
