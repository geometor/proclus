Commentary on the Definitions (I.Def.1–23) — GEOMETOR Commentary & Concordance Notes
====================================================================================

GEOMETOR Philosophical & Mathematical Commentary
------------------------------------------------

1. **Numbering of the Definitions (Euclid/Friedlein 1–23 vs. Taylor/Barocius I–XXXV)**:
   In modern critical editions of :person:`Euclid <euclid>` (Heiberg, :person:`Heath <heath>` in `geometor/euclid`, and `geometor/elements`) as well as :person:`Friedlein's <friedlein>` Greek text, Book I contains **23 Definitions** (:just:`I.Def.1 <I.def.1>`–:just:`I.Def.23 <I.def.23>`). Renaissance editors (:person:`Barocius <barocius>` 1560) and :person:`Thomas Taylor <taylor>` subdivided compound definitions—such as obtuse/acute angles, the seven species of triangles, and the five species of quadrilaterals—into **35 numbered sub-headings** (`DEFINITION I`–`XXXV`). Our Codex preserves Taylor's full text while grouping sections strictly by Euclid's canonical 23 Definitions (`sec-def-01` through `sec-def-23`) so every `:just:` link resolves directly to `euclid` and `elements`.
2. **Flux of a Point (`Friedlein 97, 6`) & Protagoras on Tangency (`Friedlein 139–140`)**:
   Both loci play key roles in `geometor/hoenen`:
   - At Caput IV, p. 107 n. 8 (`proclus:in-euclid:p97`), :person:`Hoenen <hoenen>` cites `Friedlein 97, 6` on the genetic definition of the line as :term:`flux of a point <rhysis>` (*ῥύσις σημείου*), pairing it with :person:`Geminus's <geminus>` Postulate 1 commentary (`Friedlein 185`).
   - At Caput III, p. 67 (`proclus:in-euclid:12.14` & `12.14-22`), Hoenen cites `Friedlein 139, 26–140, 7` where Proclus refutes :person:`Protagoras's <protagoras>` claim that a circle touches a ruler along a line rather than at a point, demonstrating the chasm between sensory thresholds and exact geometric limits.
3. **Horn Angles (`Friedlein 121–127`) and Non-Archimedean Magnitude**:
   Proclus's analysis of the :term:`horn angle <keratoeides>` (cited in `geometor/euclid` Vol. II, p. 40 on :just:`III.16`) foreshadows both medieval debates (Campanus, Peletier, Clavius) and modern non-Archimedean ordered fields.
