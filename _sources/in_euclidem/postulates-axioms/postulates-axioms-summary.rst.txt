Hypotheses, Postulates, Axioms & Propositional Form — Analytical Summary
========================================================================

*Scope*: **Friedlein pp. 178–207** · *Book III (Petitions, Axioms & Prop. I Prefatory Sections)*

Architectural Argument of Postulates, Axioms & Propositional Form
-----------------------------------------------------------------

Bridging Book I's Definitions and its forty-eight Propositions, *Friedlein* pp. 178–207 (Taylor Book III opening) establishes both the **epistemology of unproved geometric principles** and the **formal logic of Euclidean demonstration**:

1. **Hypotheses, Postulates, and Axioms (*Friedlein* 178–184)**: Reviewing three classical schools (:person:`Speusippus <speusippus>`/:person:`Amphinomus <amphinomus>`, :person:`Geminus <geminus>`, and :person:`Aristotle <aristotle>` in :cite:`Posterior Analytics I.10 <aristotle:anal-post:I.1-10>`), Proclus distinguishes :term:`axioms <axioma>` (self-evident cognitive truths common across sciences) from :term:`postulates <aitema>` (constructive geometric demands easily granted without complex machinery).
2. **Straightedge, Compass, and Right Angles (*Friedlein* 185–191)**:
   - :just:`Postulates 1–3 <I.post.1>` ground the entire constructive universe of straightedge and compass in the uniform, undeviating :term:`flux <rhysis>` of a point (quoting Geminus at *Friedlein* 185) and the revolution of a bounded distance around a stationary center.
   - :just:`Postulate 4 <I.post.4>` (*all right angles are equal*) reflects the determinacy of perpendicularity; Proclus also records :person:`Pappus's <pappus>` acute observation (*Friedlein* 189–190) that the converse fails if curvilinear lunular angles are admitted.
3. **Critique of the Fifth Postulate (*Friedlein* 191–193)**: In one of the most consequential passages in mathematical history, Proclus declares that :just:`Postulate 5 <I.post.5>` *ought to be struck completely from the postulates* because it lacks self-evidence, its converse (:just:`Prop. I.17 <I.17>`) is a proved theorem, and asymptotic curves (such as the hyperbola and conchoid) prove that continuous convergence alone does not entail intersection! He defers his full examination of :person:`Ptolemy's <ptolemy>` and his own attempted proofs to :just:`Prop. I.29 <I.29>` (:ref:`sec-prop-29`).
4. **Critique of the Common Notions (*Friedlein* 193–198)**: Proclus refutes :person:`Apollonius's <apollonius>` circular attempt to prove :just:`Common Notion 1 <I.cn.1>`, rejects scholiastic axiom inflation (such as *"two straight lines do not enclose a space"*), and compares :person:`Heron's <heron>` three-axiom canon with Euclid's five.
5. **Problems vs. Theorems & The Six Canonical Parts (*Friedlein* 199–207)**: Mediating the Academy debate between :person:`Speusippus <speusippus>` and :person:`Menaechmus <menaechmus>`, Proclus codifies the **six canonical parts** of a complete Euclidean proposition (*Friedlein* 203–207):
   - :term:`Πρότασις <protasis>` (*Enunciation*): states universally what is given and what is sought.
   - :term:`Ἔκθεσις <ekthesis>` (*Setting-out*): instantiates the datum in a concrete lettered figure.
   - :term:`Διορισμός <diorismos>` (*Specification*): isolates the sought conclusion on that particular figure.
   - :term:`Κατασκευή <kataskeue>` (*Construction*): adds the auxiliary lines and circles required for proof.
   - :term:`Ἀπόδειξις <apodeixis>` (*Demonstration*): deduces the target scientifically from causes and prior principles.
   - :term:`Συμπέρασμα <symperasma>` (*Conclusion*): ascends back from the lettered instance to the universal enunciation, sealed by *ὅπερ ἔδει ποιῆσαι* (Q.E.F.) or *ὅπερ ἔδει δεῖξαι* (Q.E.D.).
