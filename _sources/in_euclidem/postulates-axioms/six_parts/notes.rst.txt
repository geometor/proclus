Problems vs. Theorems & The Six Canonical Parts of a Proposition — GEOMETOR Commentary & Concordance Notes
==========================================================================================================

GEOMETOR Philosophical & Mathematical Commentary
------------------------------------------------

Mediating the Academy debate between :person:`Speusippus <speusippus>` and :person:`Menaechmus <menaechmus>`, Proclus codifies the **six canonical parts** of a complete Euclidean proposition (*Friedlein* 203–207): :term:`protasis`, :term:`ekthesis`, :term:`diorismos`, :term:`kataskeue`, :term:`apodeixis`, and :term:`symperasma`.

Friedlein Loci Cited in GEOMETOR (5 Loci in pp. 199–207)
--------------------------------------------------------

* :ref:`Friedlein 200, 10 <conc-friedlein-200-10>` (:ref:`text <sec-six-parts>`) — Speusippus and Amphinomus (school of Plato) vs. Menaechmus on whether all geometric propositions should be called 'theorems' or 'problems'.
  - ``euclid`` (``proclus:in-euclid:200.10``): Heath Vol. I, p. 19 [folio ``0033``]
* :ref:`Friedlein 200, 10–13 <conc-friedlein-200-10-13>` (:ref:`text <sec-six-parts>`) — Philosophical reason why Platonists preferred 'theorem': eternal intelligible realities do not undergo temporal generation.
  - ``euclid`` (``proclus:in-euclid:200.10-13``): Heath Vol. I, p. 33 [folio ``0047``]
* :ref:`Friedlein 202, 5 <conc-friedlein-202-5>` (:ref:`text <sec-six-parts>`) — Zenodotus (pupil of Oenopides) and Posidonius on how a problem inquires *whether* something exists while a theorem inquires *what* attribute belongs to it.
  - ``euclid`` (``proclus:in-euclid:202.5``): Heath Vol. I, p. 243 [folio ``0257``]
* :ref:`Friedlein 205, 13–15 <conc-friedlein-205-13-15>` (:ref:`text <sec-six-parts>`) — The Six Canonical Parts of a Proposition (Protasis, Ekthesis, Diorismos, Kataskeue, Apodeixis, Symperasma): which three parts are indispensable in every proposition.
  - ``euclid`` (``proclus:in-euclid:205.13-15``): Heath Vol. I, p. 132 [folio ``0146``]
* :ref:`Friedlein 207, 4–25 <conc-friedlein-207-4-25>` (:ref:`text <sec-six-parts>`) — When Ekthesis and Kataskeue may be omitted (as in universal arithmetic propositions), and how Symperasma converts the particular proof back to the universal Protasis.
  - ``euclid`` (``proclus:in-euclid:207.4-25``): Heath Vol. I, p. 131 [folio ``0145``]
