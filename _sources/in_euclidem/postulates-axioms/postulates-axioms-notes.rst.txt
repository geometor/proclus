Hypotheses, Postulates, Axioms & Propositional Form — GEOMETOR Commentary & Concordance Notes
=============================================================================================

GEOMETOR Philosophical & Mathematical Commentary
------------------------------------------------

1. **Geminus on Postulate 1 (`Friedlein 185`) in Hoenen's *De Noetica Geometriae***:
   In Caput IV, §4, p. 121 (`proclus:in-euclid:p185`), :person:`Hoenen <hoenen>` quotes `Friedlein 185` in the original Greek:
   *«ἑπόμενόν ἐστι τῷ ῥύσιν εἶναι τοῦ σημείου τὴν γραμμὴν καὶ τὴν εὐθεῖαν ὁμαλὴν καὶ ἀπαρέγκλιτον ῥύσιν»* (*"it follows from the line's being the flux of a point, and the straight line a uniform and undeviating flux"*). For Hoenen, this passage proves that ancient constructive postulates were grounded in intelligible genetic intuition of spatial continuity rather than arbitrary formalist conventions.
2. **Postulate 5 (`Friedlein 191–193`) & Epistemological Crisis**:
   In Caput I, §5, p. 15 of `geometor/hoenen`, Hoenen invokes :person:`Proclus <proclus>` (*teste Proclo*) as the primary ancient witness that :just:`Postulate 5 <I.post.5>` provoked foundational doubts from antiquity onward and that replacement postulates invariably presupposed an equivalent unproved assumption. Proclus's invocation of **asymptotic curves** (the hyperbola and Nicomedes' conchoid, `Friedlein 191–192`, cf. `177, 11`) remains a marvel of geometric insight: monotonic decrease of distance does *not* imply intersection!
3. **The Six Canonical Parts (`Friedlein 203–207`) & `geometor/elements`**:
   Proclus's six-part schema (:term:`protasis <protasis>`, :term:`ekthesis <ekthesis>`, :term:`diorismos <diorismos>`, :term:`kataskeue <kataskeue>`, :term:`apodeixis <apodeixis>`, :term:`symperasma <symperasma>`) directly informs the formal architecture of `geometor/elements` and `geometor/euclid`, where given inputs (*ekthesis*), solvability guards (*diorismos*), constructive DAG steps (*kataskeue*), and deductive justifications (*apodeixis*) are explicitly distinguished—while keeping this historical Codex strictly free of modern Python implementation code.
