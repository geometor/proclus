=======
Mission
=======

**GEOMETOR • proclus** establishes the complete, open-access digital critical edition and bidirectional citation concordance of **Proclus Lycius Diadochus's** *In Primum Euclidis Elementorum Librum Commentarii* (*Commentary on the First Book of Euclid's Elements*, c. 470 AD).

Why Proclus Anchors the GEOMETOR Canon
--------------------------------------

Across the classical canon of **GEOMETOR**, Proclus's *Commentary* serves as the indispensable historical, axiomatic, and epistemological bridge:

1. **Historical Foundation for Euclid (`geometor/euclid` & `geometor/elements`)**:
   Sir Thomas Little Heath's *Thirteen Books of Euclid's Elements* cites Gottfried Friedlein's 1873 Teubner edition of Proclus across **156 distinct page-and-line loci** (159 citing occurrences). Without Proclus, we would lack **Eudemus of Rhodes's** *History of Geometry* (*Friedlein* 64–70), **Pappus's** zero-construction proof of *I.5* (*Friedlein* 249–250), **Oenopides's** astronomical *gnomon* (*Friedlein* 283), **Thales's** ship-distance theorem (*Friedlein* 352), **Claudius Ptolemy's** treatise on the Parallel Postulate (*Friedlein* 365–368), the **Pythagorean angle-sum proof** (*Friedlein* 379), and the Pythagorean origin of **application of areas** (*parabole, hyperbole, elleipsis*, *Friedlein* 419–420).
2. **Epistemological Dialogue with Hoenen (`geometor/hoenen`)**:
   In *De Noetica Geometriae* (1954), Petrus Hoenen S.J. engages Proclus directly (`Friedlein` 12, 49, 97, 139–140, 185, 191, 365–373) on the inexactitude of sensible figures, the role of **mathematical imagination** (*φαντασία*) and **intelligible matter** (*ὕλη νοητή*), **Geminus's** definition of the straight line as uniform undeviating flux, and the epistemological crisis of the **Fifth Postulate**.
3. **Formal Logic of Constructive Geometry**:
   Proclus's codification of **The Six Canonical Parts of a Geometric Proposition** (*Protasis, Ekthesis, Diorismos, Kataskeue, Apodeixis, Symperasma*, *Friedlein* 203–207) articulates the exact logical grammar uniting synthetic Greek geometry with modern constructive verification.

Editorial Principles
--------------------

* **100% Authentic Public-Domain Sources**: Pairs Gottfried Friedlein's 1873 Teubner polytonic Greek edition (`urn:cts:greekLit:tlg4036.tlg011.opp-grc1`) with Thomas Taylor's 1788–1789/1792 English translation and fresh GEOMETOR scholarly commentary.
* **Strict Historical Separation**: Preserves the historical treatise cleanly without embedding modern Python construction scripts, linking outward via ``:just:`` to ``geometor/euclid`` and ``geometor/elements``.
