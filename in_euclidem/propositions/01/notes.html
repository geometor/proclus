Prop. I.1: Construction of the Equilateral Triangle — GEOMETOR Commentary & Concordance Notes¶

GEOMETOR Philosophical & Mathematical Commentary¶

Proclus’s commentary on Prop. I.1 (Friedlein pp. 208–219) forms an essential link in Book I’s systematic construction of plane geometry.

Friedlein Loci Cited in GEOMETOR (12 Loci in pp. 208–219)¶

  • Friedlein 208, 21 (text) — Commentary on Prop. I.1: why Euclid opens Book I with the problem of constructing the equilateral triangle. - euclid (proclus:in-euclid:208.21): Heath Vol. I, p. 130 (chapter_ix) [folio 0144]

  • Friedlein 210, 18 (text) — Symbolic and cosmic primacy of the equilateral triangle (Plato’s Timaeus) alongside its deductive role in Book I. - euclid (proclus:in-euclid:210.18): Heath Vol. I, p. 31 (chapter_iv) [folio 0045]

  • Friedlein 211, 1–212, 4 (text) — Definition of Lemma (λαμβανόμενον requiring confirmation) vs. Case (ptosis), Porism, Objection (enstasis), and Reduction (apagoge). - euclid (proclus:in-euclid:211.1-212.4): Heath Vol. I, p. 133 (chapter_ix) [folio 0147]

  • Friedlein 211, 19 (text) — Definition of geometrical Analysis (ascent to admitted principles) and Synthesis, attributed to Plato’s transmission to Leodamas of Thasos. - euclid (proclus:in-euclid:211.19): Heath Vol. I, p. 38 (chapter_iv) [folio 0052]

  • Friedlein 211, 19–23 (text) — Plato hands down the analytical method to Leodamas, through which many discoveries in geometry were made. - euclid (proclus:in-euclid:211.19-23): Heath Vol. I, p. 36 (chapter_iv) [folio 0050]

  • Friedlein 212, 5–11 (text) — Meaning of Case (ptosis): varying configurations of a construction that preserve the same demonstrative force. - euclid (proclus:in-euclid:212.5-11): Heath Vol. I, p. 134 (chapter_ix) [folio 0148]

  • Friedlein 212, 14 (text) — Dual meaning of Porism: a corollary won as a windfall from another demonstration vs. an intermediate inquiry seeking to find an existing thing (as in Euclid’s Porisms). - euclid (proclus:in-euclid:212.14): Heath Vol. I, p. 13 (chapter_ii) [folio 0027]

  • Friedlein 212, 18–23 (text) — Aristotelian Objection (enstasis) in geometry: blocking either the construction or the demonstration. - euclid (proclus:in-euclid:212.18-23): Heath Vol. I, p. 135 (chapter_ix) [folio 0149]

  • Friedlein 212, 24–213, 11 (text) — Geometric Reduction (apagoge), exemplified by Hippocrates of Chios’s reduction of duplicating the cube to finding two mean proportionals. - euclid (proclus:in-euclid:212.24-213.11): Heath Vol. I, p. 135 (chapter_ix) [folio 0149]

  • Friedlein 214, 18 (text) — Zeno of Sidon’s Epicurean critique of Prop. I.1 (what if two straight lines share a common segment?) and Posidonius’s refutation book against Zeno. - euclid (proclus:in-euclid:214.18): Heath Vol. I, p. 196 (Bk. I, first_principles) [folio 0210]

  • Friedlein 215, 24 (text) — Posidonius’s reply to Zeno of Sidon: Postulate 2 implicitly guarantees that a finite straight line can be produced in one unique straight continuation. - euclid (proclus:in-euclid:215.24): Heath Vol. I, p. 197 (Bk. I, first_principles) [folio 0211]

  • Friedlein 217, 10 (text) — Critique of attempting to prove uniqueness of straight segments from circle bisection without circularity. - euclid (proclus:in-euclid:217.10): Heath Vol. I, p. 198 (Bk. I, first_principles) [folio 0212]
