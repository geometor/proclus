Commentary on Propositions I.1–I.15: Triangles, Congruence & Perpendiculars — GEOMETOR Commentary & Concordance Notes¶

GEOMETOR Philosophical & Mathematical Commentary¶

  1. Zeno of Sidon’s Continuity & Uniqueness Critiques (`Friedlein 214–218`): In Prop. I.1, two questions arise that modern axiomatics (Pasch, Hilbert) later formalized: (a) why must the two circles intersect? and (b) as Zeno of Sidon asked, how do we know the two radii $CA, CB$ do not share a common segment near $C$? In geometor/model, exact polynomial intersection of the two unit circles $(x)^2 + y^2 = 1$ and $(x - 1)^2 + y^2 = 1$ algebraically exhibits the two conjugate equilateral vertices $(1/2, \pm \sqrt{3}/2)$ over $\mathbb{Q}(\sqrt{3})$, while Zeno’s uniqueness critique highlights the implicit uniqueness content of Postulates 1–2 defended by Posidonius.

  2. Pappus’s Zero-Construction Symmetry Proof of `I.5` (`Friedlein 249–250`): Preserved exclusively here (proclus:in-euclid:249.20-250.12), Pappus's proof of Prop. I.5 conceives the single isosceles triangle $ABC$ ($AB = AC$) simultaneously as $\triangle ABC$ and $\triangle ACB$, applying Prop. I.4 directly without a single auxiliary line—an early triumph of transformational/reflection symmetry in synthetic geometry.

  3. Trisection, Indivisible Lines & Oenopides’ Gnomon (`Friedlein 272–289`): Proclus’s distinction between straightedge-and-compass angle bisection (I.9) and general angle trisection (Friedlein 272, requiring cubic/transcendental curves—conics, conchoids, or quadratrices) anticipates Wantzel’s 1837 algebraic degree criterion ($2^k$ vs. degree $3$), while Oenopides' astronomical perpendicular (I.12, Friedlein 283, 7–10) anchors Book I’s constructive restrictions in classical celestial measurement.
